# Safety and intended use

SayAgain is assistive communication software. It helps a person find possible words and phrases using clues the person chooses to provide.

SayAgain does **not**:

- diagnose aphasia or stroke;
- provide treatment or speech therapy;
- replace medical care or a speech-language pathologist;
- read, know, or infer the user's thoughts or intended meaning.

Speech recognition and visible-object labels may be incomplete or wrong. Suggestions are possibilities, not conclusions. The person confirms every term, chooses every phrase, and must tap **Speak** before audio output. The app does not speak an unconfirmed candidate.

Pain wording is assembled only from the person's explicit body-area and severity selections. SayAgain does not infer a cause or provide treatment instructions. If the person explicitly selects the emergency option, the app displays a concise reminder to contact local emergency services; it does not assess whether an emergency exists.

No clinical effectiveness claim is made. Product research and accessibility review with people who use AAC and qualified communication professionals remain necessary before broad release.
